Can You Paint Over Damaged Drywall?

Repair holes, loose drywall paper and raised patches before painting. Paint changes the color, and the shape and texture of the wall will still show through.

Which wall damage needs repair first?

Small nail holes and shallow dents can often be filled during normal paint prep. Torn drywall paper, loose joint tape and old patches that stand proud of the wall need more attention. Look at the wall with light coming from the side. Shadows around a patch can make high and low spots easier to see.

If the drywall is soft, damp or stained from an ongoing leak, have the moisture source addressed before putting a new finish over it. Let the area dry and check the condition of the wall before deciding whether it can be patched or needs a section replaced.

What happens between patching and painting?

Once the surface is sound, the goal is to blend the repair into the surrounding wall. A patch may need a wider, tapered layer of compound so its edges disappear into the existing surface. When the compound dries, the repair is sanded, dust is removed, and bare compound is primed where needed. Primer helps even out absorption so the patch is less likely to show a different sheen after painting.

A bathroom repaint in Garden City, Michigan

On a recent Garden City bathroom project, UpLife Painting repaired damaged drywall before changing the wall color from dark sage green to Wall Street blue. We also painted the trim white. The wall repair was part of preparing the room for the new finish.

What should you send for an estimate?

Send one close-up of the damage and one photo showing the whole wall. Let us know if it is on a wall or ceiling, whether the damage keeps coming back, and which surfaces you want painted. We can discuss the repair and repaint together.
