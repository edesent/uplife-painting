What Level of Drywall Finish Do You Need Before Painting?

If you’re building or remodeling in Macomb County or the surrounding areas, decide on your drywall finish before the crew starts taping. The right finish depends on how much light hits the walls, the paint sheen you want, and how smooth you want the finished room to look.

For most rooms with flat paint and ordinary lighting, Level 4 is a practical choice. If you have large windows, a long wall with light hitting it from the side, or plans for a glossier paint finish, Level 5 is worth discussing before you finalize the estimate.

What is a Level 4 drywall finish?

With Level 4, the drywall crew tapes and coats the seams, covers fastener heads, and smooths the finished areas. The flat surface of the drywall between the seams typically remains exposed until priming.

Level 4 is common in bedrooms, finished basements, and other rooms where you’re using flat paint and the lighting is fairly even. When the joints are properly finished, it can give you a smooth painted surface without a full skim coat.

What is a Level 5 drywall finish?

Level 5 adds a thin skim coat across the entire drywall surface after the joints and fasteners have been finished. That coat helps even out the texture between the drywall paper and the areas covered with joint compound. It reduces the chance that seams and fastener spots will show through the finished paint, especially under strong side lighting.

In a two-story great room with tall windows, afternoon sunlight can travel across the wall and make subtle differences in the surface easier to see. The same thing can happen in a hallway with wall-washing lights or on a ceiling next to a bank of windows.

Can you use Level 5 in just a few rooms?

Yes. If your main living area gets strong afternoon sun but your bedrooms have even lighting, you can ask for Level 5 in the living area and Level 4 in the bedrooms. You can also specify Level 5 on one wall or ceiling. Have those areas listed in the estimate so everyone knows where the full skim coat is included.

Does Level 5 drywall still need primer?

Yes. Drywall finishing gets the surface ready for painting, and primer helps prepare the drywall and joint compound for the finish coats. If you’re comparing quotes, check whether priming and painting are included so you know who’s handling each step.
